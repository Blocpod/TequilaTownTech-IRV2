# Tequila Town Technology — Experience Upgrade Brief

## Baseline assessment

- The block-built agave is the strongest existing moment, but it is isolated from the product story.
- The four product stages look like static interface mockups. Their motion decorates rather than explains.
- Radar reads as a generic radar screen instead of a buyer-evidence workflow.
- The App reads as floating phone cards instead of a guest moving through a venue.
- Agent Swarm reads as a generic node graph instead of one mission routed to named specialists.
- Control Center reads as a conventional KPI dashboard and implies live Tequila Town metrics that the repository does not currently provide.
- The labels FIND / GUIDE / DO / DECIDE are simple, but they hide the product architecture: ACQUIRE / EXPERIENCE / OPERATE / CONTROL.
- On wide screens, the product demos occupy too little of the frame; on mobile, their smallest text becomes visual texture instead of information.
- The connecting-loop section names the tools but does not show what information moves between them.

## Oxigen benchmark findings

- The live site uses ten canvas surfaces, one sticky surface, multiple fixed layers, and Lenis scrolling.
- Its strongest pattern is a persistent visual world that changes state while the narrative advances.
- Pixel/block matter is used as a material system: landscape, tree, portrait, and icons share one visual grammar.
- Typography stays restrained while spatial transformation carries the spectacle.
- The opportunity for Tequila Town is to keep that level of continuity while adding something Oxigen does not need to do: clearly demonstrate four real product workflows.

## Chosen upgrade

Keep the existing brand, hero, and page architecture. Replace the four decorative machines with four large, scroll-driven demonstrations:

1. **Buyer Gauntlet / Radar** — candidates enter, non-buyers are rejected, source proof is checked, five fit dimensions score the buyer, the list ranks, and outreach is prepared.
2. **Living Journey / App** — a ticket unlocks entry, the venue path activates, a mission begins, a scan completes it, the passport is stamped, points rise, and the next room is personalized.
3. **Mission Switchboard / Agent Swarm** — a sponsor-recap mission enters, the orchestrator selects Analytics, Sponsor Intelligence, Research, Docs, and Slides, their work returns, and a human-ready recap emerges.
4. **Signal Refinery / Control Center** — many configured source types arrive, weak and duplicate signals fall away, verified facts rank, and three clear next moves become the daily brief.

Each sequence is reversible with scroll, readable as a static diagram with reduced motion, and independently composed for mobile.

## Visual language

- Radar: evidence paper, dark ink, sharp sorting gates, acquisition green.
- App: ticket stock, stamped paper, wayfinding blue, warm reward orange.
- Swarm: black switchboard, copper routes, named work orders, cream executive output.
- Control: cream bridge table, signal slips, strict green verification, high-priority orange.
- The loop: one physical data ribbon that visibly carries demand → guest behavior → completed work → priorities → better demand.

## Truth and claim rules

- Product animations are workflow demonstrations, not live telemetry.
- Never show Radar as contacting a lead automatically.
- Never show the App wallet or Tixr integration as production-live.
- Never show Swarm dashboard metrics or mission history as live Tequila Town data.
- Never show Control Center as already connected to Tequila Town ticket, sponsor, Radar, or Swarm feeds.
- The funding section must clearly state that the next step is connecting real systems and data.
